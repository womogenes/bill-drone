# ESP32-CAM AprilTag pose

One firmware handles onboard tag16h5 pose estimation, UART delivery to the drone, a microSD black box, Wi-Fi preview, lens calibration, and passwordless OTA. It targets the AI Thinker ESP32-CAM with a driver-detected sensor (OV3660 on this board), 4 MB flash, and PSRAM. The drone's receiver is `fw/drone/main/camera.c`.

## Wiring

| Signal | ESP32-CAM | Main ESP32 | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| CAM TX → main RX | GPIO1 (U0TXD) | GPIO34 | CAM → main |
| main TX → CAM RX | GPIO3 (U0RXD) | GPIO27 | Main → CAM (wired, unused for now) |
| SYNC | GPIO13 | GPIO13 | CAM → main |
| microSD CLK, CMD, D0 | GPIO14, 15, 2 | | on the camera board (1-bit SD mode) |
| VCC | 5V pin | 6 V | |
| GND | GND | GND | |

Signals are 3.3 V; share ground through the power wiring. Leave camera GPIO12 unconnected (a boot strap that must stay low); camera GPIO4 drives the flash LED and is held low. Main GPIO13 uses a pull-down. Camera GPIO13 is also the card's DAT3, which must be high when the card is first addressed or the card enters SPI mode, so the camera drives SYNC high while it mounts the card at boot (one long pulse the drone ignores) and low after. The camera prints nothing on UART0 after boot; the boot ROM's own messages on GPIO1 fail the drone's magic and CRC checks and are skipped. Put a 220–470 µF capacitor across the camera's 5V and GND, and keep its supply off the servos' if their current spikes reset it (check `/status` `reset` and `stalls` during a servo sweep).

## Flash and update

Copy `src/wifi.example.h` to git-ignored `src/wifi.h` and set network credentials. OTA has no password. From `src/`, build and flash the camera's programmer port the first time:

```sh
uvx --from platformio==6.1.18 --with intelhex pio run -d fw/esp32cam
uvx --from platformio==6.1.18 --with intelhex pio run -d fw/esp32cam -t upload --upload-port /dev/ttyUSB_CAM
```

For serial flashing, ground camera GPIO0 and reset, with the microSD card removed (its pull-up on GPIO2 blocks the ROM's download mode) and the drone link unplugged from GPIO1/3 (the programmer uses the same pins); release GPIO0 and reset afterwards. USB numbers move and the two adapters share a USB serial number, so identify the board first: the CAM has MAC `58:2A:BD:79:2B:9C`. After the first wired flash, check that the board boots with a card inserted: on some ESP32-CAM boards a card pulls GPIO12 high, which selects 1.8 V flash and stops the boot (the permanent fix is `espefuse.py set_flash_voltage 3.3V`).

Later updates go over Wi-Fi to `bill-esp32cam.local` (add `--upload-port <camera-ip>` without mDNS). The flash holds two 1.25 MiB OTA slots. An update pauses processing and reboots the CAM, so update while the flight controller is disarmed.

```sh
uvx --from platformio==6.1.18 --with intelhex pio run -d fw/esp32cam -e ota -t upload
```

There is no serial console after boot: UART0 is the link, ESP-IDF log output goes to `/log` (the last 2 KB), and the ROM's printf is silenced. Debug over Wi-Fi with `/status` and `/log`.

## Link and SYNC

UART0 at 2 Mbaud, 8N1: one 164-byte packet per frame (TAG3, below), 0.8 ms on the wire. Each packet is written once to the UART driver's transmit buffer, so the detector never waits for the wire; if the previous packet were still unsent the new one is dropped without a pulse and counted in `/status` `link_dropped`. SYNC goes high for at least 20 µs at the instant the camera reads its clock for the packet's `ageUs`, just before queuing it, so `ageUs` is capture-to-pulse: the drone dates the capture as its rising-edge time minus `ageUs`, independent of UART buffering and its own task latency. The drone matches a packet to the newest unused edge within 10 ms before the packet completed. The camera's RX is reserved for future messages such as gyro rates; for now received bytes are only counted (`link_rx_bytes`). `/status` also reports `link_sent`.

## Black box

With a FAT32 microSD card in the camera's slot at boot, every frame is recorded to `/blackbox/NNNN.bin` (a new file per boot, one past the highest number on the card; a file reaching 3.5 GB is closed and the next started). Without a card, or with one that does not mount, the camera runs without it; a card inserted after boot is not used. Each entry is the frame's 224-byte record (`src/record.h`, as `record.py` reads), the flow's 80×60 reduced frame, a 96×96 full-resolution crop centred on the tag when it was found (mostly from the tracker's internal-RAM copy of the tag's neighbourhood), and every Nth frame the whole 320×240 frame (N is `blackBoxFullEvery` in `config.h`, default 0, off; `GET /blackbox/control?full_every=N` changes it until reboot, and each full frame costs core 1 a 77 KB PSRAM copy). The format is in `src/blackbox.h` and at the top of `blackbox.py`: a 104-byte header (magic `BILLBBX1`, the sizes, the calibration and the build) and 16-byte entry headers (`BBE1`, flags, length, frames dropped so far).

The detector only copies the entry into a 1 MiB ring in PSRAM (about 1 ms) and never waits: if the ring is full the entry is dropped and counted, and tracking and the packet are unaffected. A priority-1 task on core 0 moves the ring to the card in 8 KB writes from a DMA-capable internal-RAM buffer (the SDMMC driver copies anything else through 512 bytes at a time), allocated only when a card mounts, and syncs the file once a second, so a power cut loses at most a second. At the default settings an entry is 14,264 bytes, 419 KB/s at 29.4 fps, 1.5 GB per hour: a 32 GB card holds about 20 hours. The bus runs at 20 MHz in 1-bit mode (2.5 MB/s raw). `/status` reports `sd` (`none`, `failed`, `idle`, `recording` or `error`), `sd_file`, `sd_frames` (entries queued in this file), `sd_dropped`, `sd_bytes` (written to the card), `sd_card_mb`, `sd_free_mb` (at the last file start), `sd_ring_lowest` (least free ring space, bytes) and `sd_write_longest_us`. Watch `internal_min` with a card in: the staging buffer and the writer's stack take about 12 KB of internal RAM.

After landing, `uv run fw/esp32cam/blackbox.py --fetch` (from `src/`) downloads new files from `GET /blackbox` (a JSON list) and `GET /blackbox/file?name=NNNN.bin` to `sw/recordings/blackbox/`; `GET /blackbox/control?record=0` closes the current file first (reading the card while recording competes with the writer), `record=1` starts a new one. `uv run fw/esp32cam/blackbox.py <file>` summarizes a file (frames, rate, drops by the black box against sequence gaps from elsewhere, crops and full frames, found and valid rates), and `--png <dir>` exports the reduced frames, crops and full frames (`--every`, `--limit`). `record.py --analyze <file>` reads black-box files as well as `.frm` recordings. `make camera-test` (from `src/fw`) checks the format round trip on the host with the camera's own encoder.

## Dashboard calibration and preview

Open **Camera** (`/camera`) in the dashboard and connect. Enter the printed **tag16h5 ID (0–29)** and **outer black-square width in millimetres**, excluding the white margin. Capture **25–40 sharp views of the same flat tag**, varying both tilt axes, distance, and position across the image while keeping all corners visible. Press Space or click Capture view; Space is ignored while typing in inputs. A single tag has only four corners, so varied views are especially important.

**Calibrate and save** runs `calibration.py` with `src/.venv/bin/python`, fits the lens, writes `src/config.h`, and sends the calibration to the CAM's `/config` endpoint. The CAM applies it immediately and stores it in NVS, where it takes precedence over the compiled defaults; no reflashing is needed. A failed camera connection leaves the local fit saved, but the dashboard reports failure until it is applied to the board.

The fit uses focal lengths, principal point, and two radial distortion coefficients; tangential distortion and k3 are fixed to zero because a single tag supplies few constraints. Reprojection RMS must be below 1 px; a small RMS alone does not prove accuracy. Check measured distances after calibration and recalibrate after changing focus, lens, resolution, crop, or orientation. Detection and preview use the same upright 320×240 raw grayscale (Y8) capture of the sensor's central 1536×1152 window, with hmirror=0 and vflip=1, and the dashboard applies no further flip. The compiled calibration was derived on 2026-10-21 from a full-window fit (focal lengths ×4/3, principal point shifted for the crop); the measured tag distance agreed within 0.4%. k2 = 0.90 from a four-corner fit is suspect, so recalibrate for best accuracy.

Preview is MJPEG on camera port 81 (`/stream`), proxied through the dashboard, at up to 15 fps. Each part carries its frame's tag in an `X-Tag` header (`none`, or `valid`/`tracked`, the four corners in 320×240 pixels and the camera position in metres), and the dashboard draws that outline over the frame, green when the pose is valid and amber when not. The default preview is the flow task's 80×60 reduction of the frame, already in internal RAM: a core-0 task copies its 4.8 KB and the low-priority HTTP task encodes it and writes the whole part in one send, so the preview never holds a frame buffer or reads PSRAM and the detector keeps the sensor's 29.4 fps. Copying even 160×120 preview pixels out of the PSRAM frame lost the next frame about half the time. `/stream?full=1` (the dashboard's full-resolution toggle, needed to capture calibration views) hands over the frame buffer itself for a 320×240 copy, which holds it about 26 ms and adds a few milliseconds to some frames' processing (p95 8 → 13 ms) at about 5 fps. Without a viewer nothing is copied or encoded.

## Pipeline and timing

The OV3660 reads the central 1536×1152 of its 2048×1536 array, binned and scaled 2.4× to 320×240 Y8, at 29.4 fps: 576 of 591 lines at 57.5 µs each (40 MHz SYSCLK, 10 MHz PCLK), so a frame takes 33 ms to read out. Against the full field this gives 4/3 the angular resolution over 36°×27°. A faster line rate (the JPEG-mode 50 MHz clock, 46 µs lines) stalled the driver's DMA copy and panicked within minutes, so 57.5 µs is the floor at 320 pixels wide; 100 fps would need 160×120 (settings in `config.h`). Core 1 runs capture, the tag search, pose and the UART packet; core 0 runs the image flow, Wi-Fi, OTA, the HTTP endpoints, the preview, the black-box writer and the driver's DMA copy.

Two frame buffers in PSRAM with `CAMERA_GRAB_LATEST`. The driver's queue is first in, first out, so more buffers only return older frames; with two, the driver stops capturing while the detector holds one and the other waits. A fetched frame more than two frame periods old is returned and the next capture used. The driver's task must empty a 12-line DMA block every 0.7 ms; it is created at the Wi-Fi task's priority, where Wi-Fi traffic cost frames (21–22 Hz with a viewer), so `startCamera` raises it to 24 (`/status` `driver_priority`), which gives 29.5 Hz with or without a viewer. About 0.3–0.5% of frames still arrive short (the driver's `FB-SIZE` error, counted in `short_frames`) and are dropped; the delivered rate is about 95% of the sensor's. A driver stall restarts the camera (up to three tries) and counts `stalls` and `recoveries`; only if all three fail does `/status` say `"camera":"stalled"`. Wi-Fi and OTA stay up either way. `last_crumbs` records, across a panic restart, which step each core was in before the last reset. The reset history survives every reset in NVS (`health.h`): `boots`, `brownouts`, `power_ons`, `panics`, `watchdogs` and `resets_by_reason` (indexed by `esp_reset_reason_t`: 1 power-on, 3 software or OTA, 4 panic, 9 brownout). A power-on reset means the supply really went away (a connector or wire, or a deliberate replug), and it scrambles `last_crumbs`; a brownout means the 3.3 V rail sagged under load. Wi-Fi reports `rssi`, `wifi_connects`, `wifi_drops`, `wifi_last_drop_reason` (`wifi_err_reason_t`) and `wifi_last_drop_ms`/`wifi_last_connect_ms` (uptime). The core's auto-reconnect retries most losses at once, and `serviceOta` calls `WiFi.reconnect()` every 5 s while still disconnected.

Exposure is fixed at 10 ms (174 lines) with automatic gain up to its 64× ceiling, so motion blur stays a third of a full-frame exposure. Every 15 frames, if the gain is above 56× the exposure grows by a quarter (up to the whole frame), and below 24× it shrinks back toward 10 ms. The sensor's own exposure control ran at the full 33 ms frame and ignored its limit registers. Measured at 1.7 m, against exposure: 33 ms needed 8.25× gain (position standard deviation 4.4, 3.2, 1.8 mm), 17 ms 15.8× (5.7, 4.1, 2.2 mm), 10 ms 26.6× (8.6, 5.9, 3.3 mm), 5 ms 55 to 62× (14, 11.6, 6 mm), all 100% valid.

Finding the tag (`tag.h`, the same code the simulator runs) never depends on where it was: tracking and the windowed detector only save time, and whenever both fail the whole frame is searched, which takes 80 ms at decimation 3 (decimation 2 took about 200 ms and found no smaller tags in simulation, down to 23 px). The whole-frame search's many small allocations go to PSRAM (`detectorWorkspace`): in internal RAM they took its minimum down to 7.7 KB, and with them in PSRAM it stays near 65 KB.

Once the tag is found, each frame re-measures it with `track.h` instead of the detector. The corners are predicted from their velocity (zero if the tag was last seen more than 0.5 s ago), then aligned with the frame: the tag's expected pattern (its code, black border and white margin, one sample per cell) is correlated with the frame at shifts up to 6 px plus the distance it could have moved (up to 40 px), half a cell apart, then refined with interpolated samples down to a pixel. A blurred tag still correlates best where it is. If that fails and the prediction had moved the tag, the alignment is retried where it was last seen. From the aligned corners, about 30 intensity profiles across each edge locate the edge to a fraction of a pixel (the step's area over its height, which is exact for any blur), a weighted line is fitted per edge, and the corners are the lines' intersections. The tag's 16 data bits are then read through the new corners and must match its code with at most one bit wrong, so every pose is measured from that frame's pixels. The tag's neighbourhood is first copied to a 24 KB internal-RAM buffer. Tracking takes 5 to 7 ms, about 15 ms when a 40 px alignment has to read the PSRAM frame. In simulation the alignment cut tracking failures from 22 to 1 (pan at 60°/s) and 114 to 6 (hand-held at 40°/s). The AprilTag detector runs only to acquire or recover the tag: in a window around the prediction and the last position when the tag was seen in the last 0.5 s, else on the whole frame, with the decimation and cluster limits sized to the tag. Its corners are shifted by half a pixel into the calibration's convention (pixel centres at integers) and then re-measured by the edge tracker; a detection the tracker cannot confirm gives no valid pose.

The pose is solved in closed form by IPPE (`pose.h`, as OpenCV's `SOLVEPNP_IPPE_SQUARE`), giving both planar solutions in about 0.1 ms with no allocation. The capture time is the readout time of the tag's centre row (rolling shutter, 138 µs per output row), or of the frame's centre row when there is no tag. Rolling shutter shears a moving tag (its top and bottom rows are read up to 6 ms apart for a 42 px tag), and a sheared square looks tilted: in simulation it made the pose pick the wrong planar solution in up to 90 frames per moving scenario. Before the pose, each corner is moved back to where it was when the tag's middle row was read, by the corners' velocity and acceleration over the last two frames, or by the frame's image flow when the tag was not seen in the previous frame (`tagPose`). A pose can be valid only if its corners are trusted (`TagFinder::trustworthy`): measured by the edge tracker, with a velocity from consecutive frames or a flow over one frame period, and the tag moving at most 30 px per frame (about 60°/s), beyond which blur and changing motion corrupt the shape. The pose waits for the flow, which usually finishes first.

The flow (`flow.h`) runs on core 0 while core 1 tracks the tag. It reduces the frame to 80×60 (one row in four, four pixels averaged), picks the 12 blocks of the previous reduced frame with the most texture in both directions outside the predicted tag, finds each in the current one by sum of absolute differences within ±4 small pixels, refines with two Lucas-Kanade steps, and reports the mean shift of the blocks within one small pixel of the median, in full-resolution pixels, with the inlier count and the median deviation. It takes about 6 ms; in simulation the shift error is 0.05 to 0.1 px.

Measured over a 35-minute soak (10 ms exposure, gain about 24×, 151 mm tag 1.70 m away, polling `/status` once a second): no stalls, no reboots, 27.9 Hz delivered, tag-row age at send median 36.5 ms (p90 40.7, max 46.1), frame fetch to packet queued median 7.0 ms, 99.9% of frames valid, every frame tracked after acquisition, reprojection RMS median 0.11 px, camera position standard deviation 8.8, 5.6 and 3.3 mm (x, y, z), flow standard deviation 0.02 and 0.03 px per frame with the camera still. Almost all of the camera-in-tag jitter is tilt noise times distance (the tag's position in the camera frame varies by tenths of a millimetre), which the drone's attitude can replace. `/status` reports `detect_us` (frame fetched to packet queued), `frame_us` (capture interval), `age_us` (tag-row age), `frame_age_us`, the tracked and detected counts, the flow, `gain`, `exposure_lines`, the lowest free internal RAM since boot (`internal_min`), `uptime_ms` and the reset reason. A packet older than 150 ms is sent with valid cleared.

## Per-frame records

`uv run fw/esp32cam/record.py` (from `src/`) subscribes to `GET /record?port=N` and saves every frame's 224-byte record (`src/record.h`: the pose packet plus the tag corners as found, the driver's frame start time, processing and flow-wait times, whether the tag was tracked or detected and whether the edge tracker measured it, why tracking failed, the alignment's correlation, the work done, whether the frame went to the preview, and the short-frame count) to `sw/recordings/camera-<time>.frm`, printing a line a second. `--seconds` stops on its own; `--analyze <file>` summarizes a recording or a black-box file: rate, sensor frames skipped, tracking losses and the corner speed before each, tracking failures by reason, position steps, reprojection, ages, the processing time per unit of work (to calibrate the simulator), and the flow against the tag's motion. It also reads the 144-byte TAG2 records from before 2026-10-22. The camera sends records over UDP (`FRM2`, six per datagram) every 100 ms, only while a subscription is fresh (5 s); the detector's cost is one record copy into a 24-slot ring in internal RAM.

## Drone receiver

`fw/drone/main/camera.c` is started at boot by the drone's `app_main`. `camera_start()` sets up UART2 at 2 Mbaud (RX GPIO34, TX GPIO27) and a rising-edge interrupt on SYNC (GPIO13), and a receiver task on core 0 aligns on the `TAG3` magic in the byte stream, checks the CRC, refuses a repeated sequence and any packet older than 150 ms on arrival, and stamps the capture time on the drone clock as the SYNC edge minus the camera's age. A packet with no unused SYNC edge in the 10 ms before it completed has no known capture time and is discarded, counted as `unsynced`; `camera_stats()` also counts skipped bytes (the camera's boot text, noise), CRC failures, buffer overflows and framing errors, plus SYNC edges and falling edges on the RX pin (a hardware pulse count, so a silent wire is told from a receiver fault). The drone's status message carries these counters and the newest frame's sequence, age and valid/trusted flags; the laptop service shows them as `reading.camera` in its `/status`. The newest frame decides: a frame without the tag clears the pose. `camera_pose(&pose, max_age_us)` returns only poses whose tilt the camera decided (valid 1 or 2). `camera_candidates(&pose, max_age_us)` also returns trusted frames whose tilt stayed ambiguous (valid 0): both planar solutions (`rotation`/`position_m` and `alt_rotation`/`alt_position_m`) and the tag's position in the camera frame (`tag_position_m`), for the drone to choose the solution that agrees with its attitude estimate; that is the only way to get a tilt at the distances where the camera cannot tell. Hover (`hover.c`) reads `camera_candidates`, using each sequence once: it flies on `tag_position_m` levelled by the IMU attitude, which the tilt ambiguity does not affect, and takes the wall's normal from the rotation only when valid is 1. `camera_flow(&flow, max_age_us)` gives the newest frame's image shift, interval and quality with its capture time on the drone clock; for velocity, subtract the rotational flow (fx or fy times the gyro rate over the interval, in the camera axes) and scale the rest by depth.

## Coordinate frames and packet

`point_tag = rotation * point_camera + position_m`. Position is the camera origin in metres in the tag frame. Rotation is a row-major 3×3 matrix, camera → tag. Camera axes are image-right, image-down, and through the lens. Tag axes are right, down, and into the printed tag in its canonical orientation. A camera facing the tag has negative tag-frame Z. Measure the camera mounting transform before body pose hold: `T_tag_body = T_tag_camera * inverse(T_body_camera)`.

Packet fields are little-endian integers and float32; Python format `<4sIIIHH16fIfHh16fHH`: magic `TAG3`, sequence, capture time (µs), age (µs), tag ID, valid, position[3], rotation[9], margin, reprojection RMS, flow[2] (px, right and down, previous frame to this), flow interval (µs, 0 without flow), flow residual (px), flow inliers, flow offset (µs, this frame's centre-row readout minus the capture time), tag position[3] (the tag centre in the camera frame, metres), the other planar solution's position[3] and rotation[9] and its reprojection RMS, trusted, CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE over 162 bytes. The margin is the detector's decision margin when it found the tag, else the tracker's mean distance of the bit samples from the black-white threshold, in grey levels.

Trusted is 1 when the corners were measured by the edge tracker with the rolling shutter removed, the tag was moving at most 30 px per frame, the reprojection RMS is at most 2 px and the packet is under 150 ms old; the pose fields are zero otherwise. The tag was acquired by exactly one detection of the configured ID with zero bit errors and decode margin at least 30 and has been tracked since with its code matching. A trusted pose is valid 1 when the second planar solution's squared error is more than twice the best one's, using a 0.3 px RMS floor below which both fit within corner noise. Otherwise the tilt is ambiguous, as it usually is for this tag beyond about 1.5 m: valid is 2 when the solution nearest the last valid pose (from the past 500 ms) is within 20° of it and the other solution is at least twice as far, and 0 if not. A frame without the tag ends the chain, so after a loss an ambiguous tilt stays invalid until an unambiguous pose. A pose resolved by continuity is only as right as the unambiguous pose that started the chain. The tag position is well determined even when the tilt is not (in simulation within a few millimetres, while a 2° tilt error moves the camera position in the tag frame by 6 cm at 1.7 m). Packets go out every frame, so the receiver sees the camera is alive. Both sides expire packets after 150 ms.

## Code layout

`main.cpp` owns capture, the camera driver, exposure and the UART link; `tag.h` finding the tag in a frame and its pose inputs; `track.h` the alignment and edge tracker; `flow.h` the image flow; `pose.h` geometry; `record.h` the per-frame records; `blackbox.h` and `sd.h` the black box; `web.h` preview, `/status` and calibration persistence; `ota.h` Wi-Fi/OTA. `build.py` builds the detector and `main.cpp` at -O2, gives the detector float math and one task per stage instead of ten, and moves its cold files to flash so Wi-Fi and camera interrupt code still fit in IRAM. Vendor code is pinned and unchanged. Bench-test calibrated accuracy and main-controller loop timing before using vision for pose hold.

## Simulator

`uv run fw/esp32cam/sim/sim.py [scenario ...]` (from `src/`) renders 320×240 frames as this camera sees them (the calibrated pinhole without distortion, rolling shutter at 138 µs per row, motion blur over the 10 ms exposure, each pixel integrating its area, lens blur and noise) of the tag on a textured wall, runs them through the camera's own `tag.h`, `track.h`, `pose.h` and `flow.h` with the AprilTag library (`sim/harness.cpp`, built with the host compiler), and scores the result against the truth. Scenarios are in `SCENARIOS` in `sim.py`: still, pans, wobbles, hand-held motion, a sweep through the whole image, near and far, off-axis, and a sudden flick. The harness charges each frame the camera time of the work it did (a cost model calibrated on the camera, in `harness.cpp`) and skips the frames the camera would miss meanwhile. Columns: track failures by reason (edge, shape, corner past reach, code unreadable, code mismatch), frames processed, frames with the whole tag in view, found and valid (of in-view frames), tracked and detected counts, wrong tilts (valid but more than 10° off; unambiguous + continuity), tilt error of the right ones (median/p95), outages, corner error (median/p95, after the rolling shutter is removed), camera position error, image speed and camera time per frame. `--exposure-us`, `--noise` and `--save <dir>` (PNG frames of the first scenario) change the rendering, `--harness` passes arguments to the harness (`--harness 2` searches whole frames at decimation 2). Rendered frames are cached in `sim/build/frames`; all scenarios take about 2 minutes on 16 cores.

With the tag in view, the moving scenarios find it in 80 to 99% of frames (77% hand-held at 80°/s) with no wrong tilts. Valid poses are fewer at the fastest motion (58% in the 120°/s pan, where the tag is often faster than 30 px per frame), and at 3 m every tilt is ambiguous: far_still is never valid.
