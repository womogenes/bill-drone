# ESP32-CAM AprilTag pose

One firmware handles onboard tag16h5 pose estimation, UART delivery to the drone, Wi-Fi preview, lens calibration, and passwordless OTA. It targets the AI Thinker ESP32-CAM with a driver-detected sensor (OV3660 on this board), 4 MB flash, and PSRAM. The drone's receiver is `fw/drone/main/camera.c`.

## Wiring

| Signal | ESP32-CAM | Main ESP32 | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| CAM TX → main RX | GPIO1 (U0TXD) | GPIO34 | CAM → main |
| main TX → CAM RX | GPIO3 (U0RXD) | GPIO26 | Main → CAM (wired, unused for now) |
| SYNC | GPIO13 | GPIO4 | CAM → main |
| VCC | 5V pin | 6 V | |
| GND | GND | GND | |

Signals are 3.3 V; share ground through the power wiring. The link is UART0 at 2 Mbaud, 8N1: the camera sends one 164-byte packet per frame (0.8 ms on the wire) and pulses SYNC high as it starts, at the moment it measured the packet's age. GPIO14, GPIO15 and GPIO2 stay free for the microSD slot in 1-bit mode. Leave camera GPIO12 unconnected (a boot strap that must stay low) and camera GPIO4 unconnected (it drives the flash LED). Main GPIO4 uses a pull-down. The camera prints nothing on UART0 after boot; the boot ROM's own messages on GPIO1 fail the drone's magic and CRC checks and are skipped. Put a 220–470 µF capacitor across the camera's 5V and GND, and keep its supply off the servos' if their current spikes reset it (check `/status` `reset` and `stalls` during a servo sweep).

## Flash and update

Copy `src/wifi.example.h` to git-ignored `src/wifi.h` and set network credentials. The local header has been populated from the repository `.env`. OTA has no password. From `src/`, build and initially flash **the camera's verified programmer port**:

```sh
uvx --from platformio==6.1.18 --with intelhex pio run -d fw/esp32cam
uvx --from platformio==6.1.18 --with intelhex pio run -d fw/esp32cam -t upload --upload-port /dev/ttyUSB_CAM
```

Ground camera GPIO0 and reset/power-cycle for serial flashing, with the microSD card removed (its pull-up on GPIO2 blocks the ROM's download mode) and the drone link unplugged from GPIO1/3; release GPIO0 and reset afterward. Identify the board before selecting a port: USB numbers can move, and these two adapters have identical USB serial numbers. The CAM identified on 2026-10-19 has MAC `58:2A:BD:79:2B:9C`, physical path `/dev/serial/by-path/pci-0000:c1:00.3-usb-0:1:1.0-port0` (then USB1). The main controller was USB0 and printed `Starting app...`. Reconfirm if wiring changes.

Subsequent firmware updates:

```sh
uvx --from platformio==6.1.18 --with intelhex pio run -d fw/esp32cam -e ota -t upload
```

Hostname: `bill-esp32cam.local`; override with `--upload-port <camera-ip>` when mDNS is unavailable. The first flash installs two 1.25 MiB OTA slots. Updates pause processing and reboot the CAM; update while the flight controller is disarmed.

## Dashboard calibration and preview

Open **Camera** (`/camera`) in the dashboard and connect. Enter the printed **tag16h5 ID (0–29)** and **outer black-square width in millimetres**, excluding the white margin. Capture **25–40 sharp views of the same flat tag**, varying both tilt axes, distance, and position across the image while keeping all corners visible. Press Space or click Capture view; Space is ignored while typing in inputs. A single tag has only four corners, so varied views are especially important.

**Calibrate and save** runs `calibration.py` with `src/.venv/bin/python`, fits the lens, writes `src/config.h`, and sends the calibration to the CAM's `/config` endpoint. The CAM applies it immediately and stores it in NVS across reboots. Saved NVS calibration takes precedence over compiled defaults. No reflashing is needed to calibrate. A failed camera connection leaves the local fit saved, but the dashboard reports failure until it is applied to the board.

The fit uses focal lengths, principal point, and two radial distortion coefficients; tangential distortion and k3 are fixed to zero because a single tag supplies few constraints. Reprojection RMS must be below 1 px; a small RMS alone does not prove accuracy. Check measured distances after calibration and recalibrate after changing focus, lens, resolution, crop, or orientation. Both detection and preview use the same upright 320×240 raw grayscale (Y8) capture of the sensor's central 1536×1152 window, with hmirror=0 and vflip=1. These settings rotate the previous image 180 degrees. The dashboard applies no additional display flip. Calibration is stored separately for this orientation; recapture views after switching to it. The preview JPEG is encoded from the detector's own frame, so calibration sees the same geometry. The compiled calibration was derived on 2026-10-21 from the 2026-10-19 full-window fit (focal lengths ×4/3, principal point shifted for the crop); the measured tag distance agreed within 0.4%. Saved calibration lives under a new NVS key for this geometry, so the old fit is not applied. Recalibrate for best accuracy; k2 = 0.90 from a four-corner fit is suspect.

Preview is MJPEG on camera port 81 (`/stream`), proxied through the dashboard, at up to 15 fps. Each part carries its frame's tag in an `X-Tag` header (`none`, or `valid`/`tracked`, the four corners in 320×240 pixels and the camera position in metres), and the dashboard draws the frame on a canvas with that outline, green when the pose is valid and amber when not. The default preview is the flow task's 80×60 reduction of the frame, which is already in internal RAM: the detector hands it over with the tag, a core-0 task copies its 4.8 KB, and the low-priority HTTP task encodes it and writes the whole part in one send. It never holds a frame buffer or reads PSRAM, and with it open the detector still runs at the sensor's 29.4 fps. `/stream?full=1` (the dashboard's full-resolution toggle, needed to capture calibration views) hands over the frame buffer itself for a 320×240 copy, which holds it about 26 ms and adds a few milliseconds to some frames' processing (p95 8 → 13 ms) at about 5 fps, though no frames were lost in testing. Without a viewer nothing is copied or encoded. The dashboard shows actual preview fps and detector Hz.

## Pipeline and timing

The OV3660 reads the central 1536×1152 of its 2048×1536 array, binned and scaled 2.4× to 320×240 Y8, at 29.4 fps: 576 of 591 lines at 57.5 µs each (40 MHz SYSCLK, 10 MHz PCLK), so a frame takes 33 ms to read out. Against the full field this gives 4/3 the angular resolution over 36°×27°. The JPEG-mode 50 MHz clock (46 µs lines) stalled the driver's DMA copy and the CAM panicked within minutes, even with the detector idle in internal RAM, so 57.5 µs is the line-rate floor at 320 pixels wide. Core 1 runs capture, the tag search, pose and the UART packet; core 0 runs the image flow, Wi-Fi, OTA, `/status`, `/config`, the preview and the driver's DMA copy.

Two frame buffers in PSRAM with `CAMERA_GRAB_LATEST`. The driver's queue is first in, first out, so more buffers only return older frames; with two, the driver stops capturing while the detector holds one and the other waits. A fetched frame more than two frame periods old is returned and the next capture used. About 0.3 to 0.5% of frames arrive short (the driver's `FB-SIZE` error: one 12-line DMA block missing) and are dropped by the driver, whatever the line rate, the flow task or Wi-Fi power saving; together with other dropped frames the delivered rate is about 95% of the sensor's. On 2026-10-21 one run of such failures lasted past the driver's 4 s timeout, a stall. Now a stall restarts the camera (up to three tries) and counts `stalls` and `recoveries` in `/status`; only if all three fail does `/status` say `"camera":"stalled"`. Wi-Fi and OTA stay up either way. Every Wi-Fi packet used to cost frames: the driver's task empties a 12-line DMA block every 0.7 ms, and it was created at priority 23, the same as the Wi-Fi task on core 0, so preview, `/status` and record traffic delayed it and lost blocks (short frames) or whole frames. With a viewer the detector got 21 to 22 Hz. `startCamera` now raises the driver's task to the top priority, 24 (`/status` `driver_priority` shows both), and on 2026-10-22 that gave 29.5 Hz with and without a viewer, with no frames skipped and no short frames in 20 s. `/log` returns the last 2 KB of ESP-IDF log output (the driver's errors), and `short_frames` counts the `FB-SIZE` errors. `last_crumbs` records, across a panic restart, which step each core was in before the last reset.

Exposure is fixed at 10 ms (174 lines) with automatic gain up to its 64× ceiling, so motion blur stays a third of a full-frame exposure. Every 15 frames, if the gain is above 56× the exposure grows by a quarter (up to the whole frame), and below 24× it shrinks back toward 10 ms. The sensor's own exposure control ran at the full 33 ms frame and ignored its exposure limit registers. Measured on 2026-10-21 at 1.7 m in the brighter light, against exposure: 33 ms needed 8.25× gain (position standard deviation 4.4, 3.2, 1.8 mm), 17 ms 15.8× (5.7, 4.1, 2.2 mm), 10 ms 26.6× (8.6, 5.9, 3.3 mm), 5 ms 55 to 62× (14, 11.6, 6 mm), all 100% valid.

Finding the tag (`tag.h`, the same code the simulator runs) never depends on where it was: tracking and the windowed detector only save time, and whenever both fail the whole frame is searched, which takes 80 ms at decimation 3 (measured on 2026-10-22; decimation 2 took about 200 ms and found no smaller tags in simulation, down to 23 px). The decimation-3 search's many small allocations go to PSRAM (`detectorWorkspace`): in internal RAM they had taken its minimum down to 7.7 KB, and with them in PSRAM it stays near 65 KB. `GET /search?frames=N` makes the next N frames forget the tag and search the whole frame, to measure that path.

Once the tag is found, each frame re-measures it with `track.h` instead of the detector. The corners are predicted from their velocity (the motion since the tag was last seen, zero if that was more than 0.5 s ago), then aligned with the frame: the tag's expected pattern (its code, black border and white margin, one sample per cell) is correlated with the frame at shifts up to 6 px plus the distance it could have moved (up to 40 px), half a cell apart on the nearest pixels, then refined with interpolated samples down to a pixel. A blurred tag still correlates best where it is. If that fails and the prediction had moved the tag, the alignment is retried where it was last seen, in case the motion stopped. From the aligned corners, about 30 intensity profiles across each edge locate the edge to a fraction of a pixel (the step's area over its height, which is exact for any blur), a weighted line is fitted per edge, and the corners are the lines' intersections. The tag's 16 data bits are then read through the new corners and must match its code with at most one bit wrong, so every pose is measured from that frame's pixels. The tag's neighbourhood is first copied to internal RAM (a 24 KB buffer). This takes 5 to 7 ms, and about 15 ms when a 40 px alignment has to read the PSRAM frame. Before the alignment, almost every tracking loss under motion was an edge missing from its profiles because the prediction was more than the profile reach (a ninth of the tag) off; in simulation it cut tracking failures from 22 to 1 (pan at 60°/s) and 114 to 6 (hand-held at 40°/s). The AprilTag detector runs only to acquire or recover the tag: in a window around the prediction and the last position when the tag was seen in the last 0.5 s, else on the whole frame, with the decimation and cluster limits sized to the tag. Its corners are shifted by half a pixel into the calibration's convention (pixel centres at integers) and then re-measured by the edge tracker; a detection the tracker cannot confirm gives no valid pose. The detector library is built at -O2 with float constants and float math calls, and with one task per stage instead of ten (`build.py`).

The pose is solved in closed form by IPPE (`pose.h`, as OpenCV's `SOLVEPNP_IPPE_SQUARE`), giving both planar solutions in about 0.1 ms with no allocation. The capture time is the readout time of the tag's centre row (rolling shutter, 138 µs per output row), or of the frame's centre row when there is no tag. Rolling shutter shears a moving tag (its top and bottom rows are read up to 6 ms apart for a 42 px tag), and a sheared square looks tilted: in simulation it made the pose pick the wrong planar solution in up to 90 frames per moving scenario. Before the pose, each corner is moved back to where it was when the tag's middle row was read, by the corners' velocity and acceleration over the last two frames, or by the frame's image flow when the tag was not seen in the previous frame (`tagPose`). A pose can be valid only if its corners are trusted (`TagFinder::trustworthy`): measured by the edge tracker, with a velocity from consecutive frames or a flow over one frame period, and the tag moving at most 30 px per frame (about 60°/s), beyond which blur and changing motion corrupt the shape. The pose is waited for until the flow is done (the flow usually finishes first).

The flow (`flow.h`) runs on core 0 while core 1 tracks the tag. It reduces the frame to 80×60 (one row in four, four pixels averaged), picks the 12 blocks of the previous reduced frame with the most texture in both directions outside the predicted tag, finds each in the current one by sum of absolute differences within ±4 small pixels, refines with two Lucas-Kanade steps, and reports the mean shift of the blocks within one small pixel of the median, in full-resolution pixels, with the inlier count and the median deviation. It takes about 6 ms; in simulation the shift error is 0.05 to 0.1 px.

Measured on 2026-10-21 over a 35-minute soak in the brighter light (10 ms exposure, gain about 24×), 151 mm tag 1.70 m away, polling `/status` once a second from another host: no stalls, no reboots, 27.9 Hz delivered (the sensor runs 29.4 fps), tag-row age at send median 36.5 ms (p90 40.7, max 46.1), frame-start age median 40.9 ms, frame fetch to packet queued median 7.0 ms, 99.9% of frames valid, every frame tracked after acquisition, tracker margin about 46, reprojection RMS median 0.11 px, camera position standard deviation 8.8, 5.6 and 3.3 mm (x, y, z), flow standard deviation 0.02 and 0.03 px per frame with the camera still. An earlier 35-minute soak at the sensor's full-frame exposure also had no stalls. Almost all of the camera-in-tag jitter is tilt noise times distance (the tag's position in the camera frame varies by tenths of a millimetre across), which the drone's attitude can replace. `/status` reports `detect_us` (frame fetched to packet queued), `frame_us` (capture interval), `age_us` (tag-row age), `frame_age_us`, the tracked and detected counts, the flow, `gain`, `exposure_lines`, the lowest free internal RAM since boot (`internal_min`), `uptime_ms` and the reset reason. A packet older than 150 ms is sent with valid cleared.

Faster frame rates were tried on 2026-10-21 and dropped. At 10 MHz PCLK a 320-pixel line needs 32 µs, so 100 fps requires 160×120: with 80 MHz SYSCLK and 1300-cycle lines the sensor delivered 101.5 Hz (detection off), with 21-pixel tags and half the resolution. In the room's light at that time its 9.6 ms exposure detected nothing, and whole-frame searches with frame buffers in DRAM drove internal RAM down to 5 KB and panicked. `config.h` records those settings.

## Per-frame records

`uv run fw/esp32cam/record.py` (from `src/`) subscribes to `GET /record?port=N` and saves every frame's 224-byte record (`src/record.h`: the pose packet plus the tag corners as found, the driver's frame start time, processing and flow-wait times, whether the tag was tracked or detected and whether the edge tracker measured it, why tracking failed, the alignment's correlation, the work done (tracker runs, alignment shifts, decimated pixels searched), whether the frame went to the preview, and the short-frame count) to `sw/recordings/camera-<time>.frm`, printing a line a second. `--seconds` stops on its own; `--analyze <file>` summarizes a recording: rate, sensor frames skipped, tracking losses and the corner speed before each, tracking failures by reason, position steps, reprojection, ages, the processing time per unit of work (to calibrate the simulator), and the flow against the tag's motion. It also reads the 144-byte records from before 2026-10-22. The camera sends records over UDP (`FRM2`, six per datagram) every 100 ms, only while a subscription is fresh (5 s); the detector's cost is one record copy into a 24-slot ring in internal RAM.

## Drone receiver

`fw/drone/main/camera.c` is built into the drone firmware but not started. `camera_start()` sets up UART2 at 2 Mbaud (RX GPIO34, TX GPIO26) and a rising-edge interrupt on SYNC (GPIO4), and a receiver task on core 0 aligns on the `TAG3` magic in the byte stream, checks the CRC, refuses a repeated sequence and any packet older than 150 ms on arrival, and stamps the capture time on the drone clock as the SYNC edge minus the camera's age. A packet with no SYNC edge in the 5 ms before it (SYNC unwired or missed) is still used, with its send time estimated from its arrival, and counted as `unsynced`. The newest frame decides: a frame without the tag clears the pose. Control reads `camera_pose(&pose, max_age_us)`, which returns only poses whose tilt the camera decided (valid 1 or 2), and uses each sequence once. `camera_candidates(&pose, max_age_us)` also returns trusted frames whose tilt stayed ambiguous (valid 0): both planar solutions (`rotation`/`position_m` and `alt_rotation`/`alt_position_m`) and the tag's position in the camera frame (`tag_position_m`), for the drone to choose the solution that agrees with its attitude estimate (gravity, with the tag's known mounting). That is the only way to get a tilt at the distances where the camera cannot tell. `camera_flow(&flow, max_age_us)` gives the newest frame's image shift, interval and quality with its capture time on the drone clock; for velocity, subtract the rotational flow (fx or fy times the gyro rate over the interval, in the camera axes) and scale the rest by depth (the tag's z, or another estimate). `camera_stats()` gives link counters.

## Coordinate frames and packet

`point_tag = rotation * point_camera + position_m`. Position is the camera origin in metres in the tag frame. Rotation is a row-major 3×3 matrix, camera → tag. Camera axes are image-right, image-down, and through the lens. Tag axes are right, down, and into the printed tag in its canonical orientation. A camera facing the tag has negative tag-frame Z. Measure the camera mounting transform before body pose hold: `T_tag_body = T_tag_camera * inverse(T_body_camera)`.

UART 2 Mbaud 8N1, exactly 164 bytes per frame, starting at a SYNC edge. Packet fields are little-endian integers and float32; Python format `<4sIIIHH16fIfHh16fHH`: magic `TAG3`, sequence, capture time (µs), age (µs), tag ID, valid, position[3], rotation[9], margin, reprojection RMS, flow[2] (px, right and down, previous frame to this), flow interval (µs, 0 without flow), flow residual (px), flow inliers, flow offset (µs, this frame's centre-row readout minus the capture time), tag position[3] (the tag centre in the camera frame, metres), the other planar solution's position[3] and rotation[9] and its reprojection RMS, trusted, CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE over 162 bytes. (`TAG2`, until 2026-10-22, was the first 96 bytes, a reserved zero and the CRC.) The margin is the detector's decision margin when it found the tag, else the tracker's mean distance of the bit samples from the black-white threshold, in grey levels. Trusted is 1 when the corners were measured by the edge tracker with the rolling shutter removed (see Pipeline), the tag was moving at most 30 px per frame, the reprojection RMS is at most 2 px and the packet is under 150 ms old; the pose fields are zero otherwise. The tag was acquired by exactly one detection of the configured ID with zero bit errors and decode margin at least 30 and has been tracked since with its code matching. A trusted pose is valid 1 when the second planar solution's squared error is more than twice the best one's, using a 0.3 px RMS floor below which both fit within corner noise. Otherwise the tilt is ambiguous, as it usually is for this tag beyond about 1.5 m: valid is 2 when the solution nearest the last valid pose (from the past 500 ms) is within 20° of it and the other solution is at least twice as far, and 0 if not. A frame without the tag ends the chain, so after a loss an ambiguous tilt stays invalid until an unambiguous pose. A pose resolved by continuity is only as right as the unambiguous pose that started the chain. The tag position is well determined even when the tilt is not (in simulation within a few millimetres, while a 2° tilt error moves the camera position in the tag frame by 6 cm at 1.7 m). Packets go out every frame, so the receiver sees the camera is alive. Capture time is the readout of the tag's centre row on the CAM clock (the driver stamps the start of the frame; each output row follows 138 µs later); exposure ended at that readout, and the pose is of that moment. Age is capture-to-ready on the CAM; the receiver adds how long READY was high and the transfer. Both sides expire packets after 150 ms.

`main.cpp` owns capture, the camera driver, exposure and the UART link; `tag.h` finding the tag in a frame and its pose inputs; `track.h` the alignment and edge tracker; `flow.h` the image flow; `pose.h` geometry; `web.h` owns preview and calibration persistence; `ota.h` owns Wi-Fi/OTA. `build.py` builds the detector and `main.cpp` at -O2, gives the detector float math and one task per stage, and moves its cold files to flash so Wi-Fi and camera interrupt code still fit in IRAM. Vendor code is pinned and unchanged. Bench-test calibrated accuracy and main-controller loop timing before using vision for pose hold.

## Simulator

`uv run fw/esp32cam/sim/sim.py [scenario ...]` (from `src/`) renders 320×240 frames as this camera sees them (the calibrated pinhole without distortion, rolling shutter at 138 µs per row, motion blur over the 10 ms exposure, each pixel integrating its area, lens blur and noise) of the tag on a textured wall, runs them through the camera's own `tag.h`, `track.h`, `pose.h` and `flow.h` with the AprilTag library (`sim/harness.cpp`, built with the host compiler), and scores the result against the truth. Scenarios (`SCENARIOS` in `sim.py`): still, pans at 30, 60 and 120°/s, a wobble, hand-held motion at 40 and 80°/s, a slow sweep through the whole image, hand-held near a corner, at 0.7 m and at 3 m, still at 3 m, a wobble 50° off the tag's normal, and a sudden 12° flick. The harness charges each frame the camera time of the work it did (a cost model calibrated on the camera on 2026-10-22, in `harness.cpp`) and skips the frames the camera would miss meanwhile. Columns: track failures by reason (edge, shape, corner past reach, code unreadable, code mismatch), frames processed, frames with the whole tag in view, found and valid (of in-view frames), tracked and detected counts, wrong tilts (valid but more than 10° off; unambiguous + continuity), tilt error of the right ones (median/p95), outages, corner error (median/p95, after the rolling shutter is removed), camera position error, image speed and camera time per frame. `--exposure-us`, `--noise` and `--save <dir>` (PNG frames of the first scenario) change the rendering, `--harness` passes arguments to the harness (`--harness 2` searches whole frames at decimation 2). Rendered frames are cached in `sim/build/frames`; all scenarios take about 2 minutes on 16 cores.

On 2026-10-22, against the code before (whole-frame searches at decimation 2, a tracker without alignment, no rolling-shutter correction, detector corners trusted for poses), with the tag in view: found went from 50 to 97% of frames in the moving scenarios (13% hand-held at 80°/s) to 80 to 99% (77%), whole-frame searches from about 200 to 80 ms, and wrong tilts from 2 to 78 per moving scenario to none in any. Valid poses are fewer at the fastest motion (58% in the 120°/s pan, where the tag is often faster than 30 px per frame), and at 3 m every tilt is ambiguous: far_still is never valid, and far_hand's 53% rests on a chain started by one barely unambiguous frame.
