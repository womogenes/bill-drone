# Bicopter simulation

## Code and operation

Firmware and simulation share the C attitude estimator, controller and thrust table in `fw/drone/main/flight_math.c`; `sw/sim/flight.py` wraps it for the simulation and adds thrust conversion. The controller receives accelerometer and gyro readings. It uses fixed geometry and inertia estimates; position and altitude are recorded for evaluation.

## Thrust calibration

The thrust table uses three October 4 sweeps (the CSVs and `compare.py` are kept on the `claude/slop-archive` branch under `src/sw/slop/`), calibrated with the 1.62 kg bottle comparison. `compare.py` excludes the two confirmed glitches at throttle 750. It calculates a median at each throttle for each run; the shared table uses the median across runs and interpolates between those values.

Both ESCs were recalibrated to 1000–2200 µs at 2026-10-28 06:40 UTC and the firmware table rescaled by guess (throttle c gives what 200 + 0.75 c did; not re-measured). Recordings from before then were flown with the earlier table: `flight.thrust_table(recording)` picks a recording's table by its timestamp (both tables live in `flight.py`), and everything that replays logs uses it (identify_airframe and what builds on it, `sw/fit.py`, `sw/fit_actuator.py`). With the wrong table the replayed controller misses the logged throttles by 5–18 units on average; with the right one by 0.26 (rounding) and the servos by 0.005°. Flights from both eras cannot be fitted together.

Throttle 200 is treated as approximately zero thrust, based on the bench observation. The curve retains the measured decline above 1100. Commands below 200 produce zero modeled thrust; commands above 1200 hold the last table value. Converting thrust back to throttle uses the rising branch, capped at 1100. Both motors use the same curve. Battery voltage and propeller configuration were not recorded in those CSVs.

## Sensor and actuator response

The MPU settings match `fw/drone/main/flight_rt.c`: 100 Hz sampling, ±2 g acceleration, ±250°/s gyro range, and DLPF setting 3 (44 Hz accel / 42 Hz gyro bandwidth). The [InvenSense specification, sections 6.1–6.2](https://www.haoyuelectronics.com/Attachment/GY-521/mpu6050.pdf) gives noise densities of 0.005°/s/√Hz for the gyro and 400 µg/√Hz for acceleration. The simulation estimates RMS noise as density × √bandwidth and approximates sensor latency with a 5 ms filter. It also applies ADC quantization and clipping.

Gyro bias is `[0.02, 1.10, -1.95]` °/s, from an earlier stationary dashboard observation. The simulation averages the first 200 stationary samples to estimate that bias. Accelerometer offsets are zero because tilt and bias were not measured independently. Powered logs contain both sensor noise and mechanical motion. Motor vibration, temperature drift, and sampling jitter are omitted.

Actuator parameters are estimates:

- Thrust-command delay: 20 ms, followed by a 50 ms response time constant.
- Servo play: ±0.5°, implemented by holding the target until the command takes up the slack. Load-driven movement inside the gear clearance is omitted.
- Servo PWM is 100 Hz (was 50 Hz until 2026-10-21). Servo command filter: 60 ms; stiffness: 5 N·m/rad; damping: 0.15 N·m·s/rad.
- Servo torque limit: ±0.8 N·m; travel: ±60°.
- Maximum thrust: approximately 11.22 N per motor, from the calibrated curve.
- Propeller reaction torque: ±0.015 N·m per newton of thrust, with opposite spins. The actual left/right spin assignment still needs confirmation.

The thrust sweeps contain settled readings after a one-second wait, so they do not identify motor response timing. Battery sag, aerodynamic drag, and propeller gyroscopic effects are omitted. Propeller geometry supplies approximate inertia and a visual outline. Ground contact uses simple component shapes and foot pads. The standalone XML viewer includes actuator lag; `sweep.py` adds command delay, backlash and sensor noise.

## Controller tuning

Roll P/damping are 20/6; pitch P/damping are 20/10. Yaw-rate damping is 4. Roll and pitch integral gains are 3, with correction limited to ±3 rad/s² and cleared when stopped. Pitch trim is −3.6°; left/right servo trims are +0.9°/−0.9°. These values were selected from simulated trajectories and need hardware checks.

Accelerometer correction has an approximately 1 s time constant while unpowered and 50 s while powered. Slow correction reduces the effect of horizontal acceleration being interpreted as tilt. Residual gyro bias can still cause drift. Collective returns to nominal after the takeoff pulses; the controller does not hold altitude or position.

## Measured plant (2026-10-21)

`bicopter.xml` carries the bench deck (about 5 g, as boxes) and an explicit inertial on the drone body: the geoms place the parts, while mass and inertia are set so the vehicle matches measurements. The CoM is 17.2 mm above the tube (hang test with three string lines, 17.0 mm with the old deck, plus the lighter deck), roughly centred fore/aft, so the tilt pivots sit 20.0 mm above it. Pitch inertia is 0.00157 kg·m², from flight fits that showed pitch responding about 1.4x the controller's model (0.020 m pivot, 0.0023 kg·m²). Total mass is 881 g, estimated from the 900 g weighing minus the old deck; weigh it again to confirm. Other fitted plant values: thrust about 1.09x the table at 16 V (liftoff near throttle 620-650), the left motor about 6% weak (roll_bias 3.7 rad/s² holds it), servo dead time about 40 ms (bench, battery-powered servos) plus about 60 ms response. Servo play is still unmeasured. `sweep.py`'s NOMINAL plant uses the same values.

## Sweep plant (2026-10-22)

`sweep.py` runs a 6-DOF rigid body: thrust and servo tilt in the body frame, gravity, quadratic drag (0.03 N/(m/s)², a rough estimate), and four spring-damper feet at the CAD landing-leg contact points (x ±55 mm, y ±78 mm, 104 mm below the CoM; 2000 N/m and 30 N·s/m per foot, friction 0.5). The controller sees the firmware attitude estimator fed with simulated, noisy gyro and accelerometer readings instead of the true attitude. A plant's `ramp` raises collective to T over that many seconds on the ground. On the 2026-10-21 liftoff and free-flight sweeps this matches the earlier attitude-only plant to about 1° of peak pitch and the same stable/unstable cases: the feet cannot tip the drone before liftoff (rocking about a foot takes about 0.5 N·m; a 10 rad/s² pitch push is 0.016 N·m), and the estimator error is small next to the servo lag. Collective has no altitude hold, so after the climb pulse the drone keeps climbing or sinking; free flight starts at 50 m so the floor never ends a run.

## Airframe identified from the 2026-10-27 hover flights (both rubber bands on)

`identify_airframe.py` fits `airframe.py` to the 34 flights (215 s airborne) by closed-loop multi-step prediction: the firmware controller, replayed from arming on each log (it reproduces the logged throttles to rounding and servo commands to 0.04°), flies the model from every start on the logged references alone, and the fit is least squares on rates and attitude 50 ms to 2 s ahead. Fitting to the logged commands instead (open loop), or one step ahead, credits the controller's reactions to the airframe and gave unphysical stiffness and damping and a near-zero yaw effectiveness that the impulse response contradicts.

What it found, in parts that should outlast a geometry change:

- Motors: the left motor responds after about 60 ms with almost no lag; the right one after about 115 ms with a 135 ms lag, and needs 6 % more table thrust in hover. Two different parameterisations both demanded the asymmetry, and the throttle split agrees, so it is probably the right motor, ESC or prop, but closed-loop data cannot fully separate it from the throttles' anti-correlation; a bench step of each motor would settle it. Thrust barely depends on battery voltage beyond what the runtime's compensation already does (voltage power ~0).
- Servos: about 28 ms dead time, then a second-order loop at about 37 rad/s with damping 0.8 and a slew limit near 340°/s; play 0.5° left and 0.15° right.
- Rubber bands: a forward preload on each motor assembly of about 2000°/s² (no measurable spring rate). Together with the play and the servo compensation they reproduce the drone's 3.5 Hz pitch wobble.
- Motor assemblies: their inertia about the servo axis is about 0.2 of the body's pitch inertia, so swinging the motors pitches the body the other way first. This is why the pitch response to a setpoint step starts the wrong way.
- Effectiveness against the controller's model (pivot_height 0.02-0.03, CAD inertias): roll 1.34, pitch thrust tilt 0.65, yaw 0.56. Roll also sees rate damping of about -18/s and an attitude stiffness of about -13/s² that no single physical term explains.
- Translation: thrust through the logged attitude and modelled tilts explains 70-80 % of the camera's velocity change over 0.25-1 s with a gain within 10 % of 1; rotor drag about 0.6/s forward and 0.27/s sideways.
- Disturbance: below 2 Hz the one-step residual is equivalent to 0.89 rad/s² RMS in roll and 0.49 in pitch (0.5 s correlation). Most of pitch's one-step residual (11 rad/s² RMS) is at 10-50 Hz and does not move the attitude; earlier sims' 2-5 rad/s² gusts stood in for a wrong plant.

Held out by flight (5 groups, each predicted by a fit to the others), with the controller in the loop on the logged references:

| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 ms | 0.51 | 0.66 | 0.68 | 0.70° | 1.01° | 0.83 |
| 250 ms | 0.45 | 0.52 | 0.54 | 1.22° | 2.58° | 0.63 |
| 500 ms | 0.34 | 0.46 | 0.57 | 1.41° | 3.94° | 0.71 |

A collective-thrust roll term (roll_lift) improves roll to 0.9° a second ahead, but it needs a -129 rad/s² push to cancel it at hover, has no physical reading, and in the hover simulation rolls the drone off the tag at liftoff; it is left out. With the controller replaced by the logged commands (open loop) the same model's pitch error grows to 15° in a second: pitch is only predictable with the controller in the loop.

### Stability margin and liftoff

The identified sim flies the 2026-10-27 06-42-15 settings (pitch_p 30, pitch_damping 12) badly (pitch RMS about 7°) but does not diverge, where the real hop diverged at about 2.6 Hz within 0.8 s of liftoff; p20/d8 and p30/d8 flew in both. Added servo delay does not change that (even +60 ms leaves p30/d12 mostly up), nor does white torque noise at the one-step residual's level (it degrades all three settings alike). Pitch effectiveness does: at ×1.5 p30/d12 diverges in two of three seeds, at ×2 in all within about 2 s, and p20/d8 and p30/d8 stay up at ×2 (p30/d8 fails at ×2.5). Flown closed loop from liftoff on the logs themselves, ×1.25-1.5 matches the diverged hop's growth (pitch-rate RMS 210°/s in its last 0.35 s against 208 logged; 133 at ×1).

The multi-step cost explains why one fit does not give that: over the first second after liftoff, predictions 50-250 ms ahead are best at ×1.25-1.4 the fitted pitch effectiveness, while in steady hover every horizon is best at ×0.9-1. Without the diverged hop (45 flights, or only the 30 airborne over 2 s) the first second after liftoff still prefers ×1.25-1.4 at 50-250 ms. Independently, the 2026-10-25 09-01 hop (p20/d14, right band only) oscillated in the air with pitch-rate RMS growing 60→200°/s over 3.4 s; the identified sim holds p20/d14 at 9.5° RMS at ×1 and loses it at 2.9-3.1 s at ×1.5. The airframe is stiffer in pitch right after liftoff than in hover (cause not identified: the liftoff collective's thrust is already modelled), and every divergence so far started there. `tune_gains.py` therefore demands stability with pitch and roll effectiveness ×2 (the conventional 6 dB gain margin, which here is about ×1.4 for liftoff and ×1.4 for the rest), against which the 06-42-15 settings fail and the flown p20/d8 ones pass.

The fitter now also uses hops from 0.5 s airborne (scoring each start only at horizons still airborne), which adds the short and diverged hops: 46 flights and 223 s for 2026-10-27, and refitting from the saved model moved no parameter materially. All lanes' controllers step in one C call (`flight_many.c`), so a fit takes about 4 minutes on 31 workers instead of 1.5 hours.

Held out by flight with the short and diverged hops added, the refit predicts as before (1 s ahead: roll 1.35°, pitch 3.64°; 100 ms: 0.69°, 1.02°).

Tuning from the 06-42-15 settings, searching each gain from ÷8 to ×8 of its flown value (40 generations): without the margin variants the optimum is pitch_p 58, pitch_damping 8, roll_p 197, which the margin rejects. With them it is pitch_p 38, pitch_damping 7.9, pitch_i 36, roll_p 189, roll_damping 2.6, roll_i 55, hover_kp 3.8, hover_kd 1.2, hover_kside 0.37, hover_kyaw 1.45 (worst case 0.57 against 17.8 for the flown settings, which crash at pitch ×2). Roll has no observed divergence to calibrate against and the data never flew roll_p much above 40, so roll_p 189 is an extrapolation of the model, not a recommendation; step gains out from what has flown and refit between steps.

## How far the sims are validated (2026-10-28)

All checks use only the recorded flights; nothing here is a new physical measurement.

**Airframe model (airframe.py, identify_airframe.py).** Identified by closed-loop multi-step prediction from the four hovers after the 2026-10-28 ESC recalibration (170 s airborne). Started from each airborne sample, the firmware controller flies the model on the logged references alone. Held out by flight, attitude-change error roll/pitch: 0.11°/0.58° at 50 ms, 1.08°/3.56° at 250 ms, 1.61°/4.07° at 500 ms, 1.58°/3.05° at 1 s (the previous fit: 0.18°/0.56°, 1.48°/4.10°, 1.89°/5.09°, 1.82°/3.19°). Two changes got there. A pitch torque from the tilted thrust that builds up with a 0.74 s lag and opposes about 30% of the direct tilt torque (pitch_wake −0.21, wake_tau): below 0.3 Hz the old model's pitch residual fell 0.8 rad/s² per degree of tilt, so a held tilt does less than a swung one (cause not identified: wake, ground or slow disturbance). And the band stiffness was freed (−29). Every other candidate scored within the optimizer's noise or worse: tilt-dependent effectiveness, a voltage pitch torque, asymmetric servo slew/damping/link, damping on the motor assemblies, and the bench servo dynamics as a start (the fit stalls there). Diagnosis: the 10–50 Hz part of the one-step pitch residual (about 22 rad/s² RMS) is vibration that does not move the attitude; the logged pitch motion is mostly the controller answering unmodelled slow torque, so these hovers carry little plant information above about 2 Hz, and giving the model each start's local slow torque would take pitch error at 250/500 ms to 3.4°/3.2°.

The refit also moved roll, and the roll data cannot settle it. Roll effectiveness is 0.43 with 48/64 ms motor lag, where the previous fit had 0.93 with 110/122 ms (and 2026-10-27's, before the ESC recalibration, 1.34). Each pairs with its own damping and they predict hover nearly alike (held to the previous roll values, the fit cannot improve at all: cost 2403 against 2072). For hover-sized commands only the combination matters, but righting and flipping are set by the authority, so recover.randomization spans roll effectiveness ×0.8–2.2 around the new value, covering both fits. Held at the previous roll values, the tuned recovery and flip were unchanged by the pitch term and the refit disturbances. Disturbances refit on this model (fit_disturbances.py) and calibrated as before so the camera hovers' statistics match (models/disturbances_2026-10-28_calibrated.json: roll ×0.9, pitch ×1.1 and yaw ×0.6 of the fitted torque drifts with 0.7 s correlation in roll and pitch, force drifts ×0.5): range error RMS 0.21 against 0.20 m, its fading time 1.8 against 2.0 s, sideways 0.18 against 0.17 m fading in 1.9 against 1.8 s, pitch rate 22.6 against 23.4 deg/s and roll rate 9.0 against 9.7.

**Geometry-explicit airframe (airframe.py, 2026-10-28, later).** The airframe model now takes the geometry as an input (`models/geometry_<day>.json`; `airframe.derive_geometry` from a parts list, `cad_geometry` from bicopter.xml's parts, `shifted` to carry a CAD change onto the measured geometry) and every body term is a torque from a coefficient meant not to change with the chassis, divided by the geometry's inertia; sweep.py and rigid.py take mass and inertia from it too. Before, the terms were rad/s² at the controller model's geometry (flight.MODEL) times fitted effectiveness, so a chassis change meant editing the controller's model or refitting.
- Thrust: each flight's thrust level is pinned to the weight (the table reads 6% low at 940 g after the recalibration: level 1.06, per flight 0.97–1.08), and differential_slope is the share of a differential throttle step's table thrust the motors deliver (roll authority; the common part follows the table).
- Link and bands are torques on the motor assembly's effective inertia (CAD inertia, mass × CoM offset × pivot height, and a fitted reflected servo inertia, 1.8e-4 kg·m², 1.6× the CAD part): link 2.2 N·m/rad, left band 0.076 N·m.
- The slipstream: yaw from differential tilt is only about 30% of the geometric torque and the forward push from tilt far below g, which a share wash of the tilted thrust's horizontal force cancelled on the airframe explains with one number: wash 0.74, acting 24 mm above the tube (10 mm above the CoM), so the tilt's pitch arm is 15.6 mm of the 23.4 mm pivot height at the CAD pitch inertia, and a held tilt keeps 1 − 0.74 of that (pitch_wake, lag 1.05 s).
- Props: one reaction-torque ratio for yaw and the tilted roll part with the physical sign (−0.009 m; the bench guess is 0.015; the previous separate roll coefficient had the opposite sign to yaw's) and a gyroscopic coefficient scaling with √T (fitted −0.0056 N·m·s/√N, the sign opposite to expectation: hover does not pin it).
- Rotors: roll damping as inflow 0.25 s/m at the arm (momentum theory gives about 0.07, the lag and authority are traded against it), pitch and yaw damping as one edgewise H-force coefficient 0.06 s/m at the rotor height and at the arm.
- Lumped: attitude stiffness as effective lengths times the thrust (roll 28 mm, pitch 4 mm; free flight has no attitude spring, cause not identified), speed moments as the fitted drag acting 15 mm (forward) and 42 mm (sideways) above the tube, cross-coupling as torques. com_x is calibrated by the hovers to +0.2 mm (the hang test's −2.1 mm is absorbed by the pushes).
